Lord Howe Island secrets

About this spot

Situated in the Tasman Sea, Lord Howe limits tourist numbers to just 400 at any time. It boasts world-class snorkeling around the southernmost coral reef on Earth.

Insider tips

  • Hike Mt. Gower with a mandatory local guide to see ancient cloud forests.
  • Hand-feed wild kingfish right at Ned's Beach at low tide.

Free access edition · Victoria city guide

Melbourne: culture, coffee & hidden laneways

Australia's cultural, culinary, and coffee capital. A comprehensive free guide to hidden graffiti laneways, legendary espresso bars, heritage markets, coastal penguin rookeries, and urban gems.

About Melbourne

Constantly celebrated as one of the world's most beautiful cities, Melbourne is known for its sport, coffee, and culture. Famous for its specialty coffee, architecture, and thriving live music and sports scenes, it's a city designed for curious walkers.

Top things to do in Melbourne

A curated selection of the best experiences across the city — accessible easily on foot and by tram:

CBD laneways & arcades

Explore the iconic laneways & street art

Melbourne's soul lives in the labyrinth of bluestone alleys. Wander through Hosier Lane and AC/DC Lane to experience changing masterpieces and stencils by world-class street artists. Slip into Centre Place and Degraves Street for espresso bars, and marvel at the 19th-century mosaic floors of the historic Block Arcade.

Hosier Lane & Centre Place Free Best explored on foot
World-class roasteries

Immerse yourself in the world's best coffee culture

Melbourne considers specialty coffee an art form. Skip the fast food chains and sample local roasteries. Order a "Magic" (a Melbourne original: double ristretto topped with velvety steamed milk) at CBD icons like Patricia Coffee Brewers or Brother Baba Budan, or browse the bohemian roasteries along Brunswick Street in Fitzroy.

CBD, Fitzroy & Carlton Must try: "a Magic" Third-wave roasters
Lakeside sanctuary

Stroll the Royal Botanic Gardens

Covering 38 hectares beside the Yarra River, these lush heritage gardens feature over 8,500 plant species from around the globe. Explore the arid Guilfoyle Volcano or rest beside the serene Ornamental Lake.

South Yarra / Birdwood Ave Free general admission 3.8 km Tan Track loop
Culinary haven since 1878

Feast at the historic Queen Victoria Market

Operating since 1878, "Queen Vic" is the culinary heart and soul of Melbourne. Wander through the heritage Deli Hall for fresh Coffin Bay oysters, regional farmhouse cheeses, and artisan charcuterie, and get hot jam doughnuts fresh from the vintage 1950s food van outside.

Queen St, Melbourne CBD Famous hot jam doughnuts Fresh local produce
Bayside escape

Watch fairy penguins at St Kilda beach & pier

Jump on the Route 96 or 16 tram to coastal St Kilda. Walk along the seaside pier around twilight to spot wild Little Blue Penguins. After that, explore the shops along Acland Street and pass the bright entrance of historic Luna Park.

St Kilda Pier & Acland St Twilight penguin viewing Tram route 96 or 16
Galleries & riverside walks

Explore the Arts Precinct & Southbank promenade

Cross the Princes Bridge to Southbank. Discover the National Gallery of Victoria (NGV) — Australia's oldest and most visited public gallery, with free general admission to its permanent collections. In the evening, enjoy the Yarra River promenade with waterfront dining, city skyline reflections, and the Crown gas brigade fire displays.

St Kilda Rd & Southbank Free NGV permanent exhibits Night skyline views
Albert Park & lakeside detour

Side note: Formula 1 at Albert Park Lake

If you're a motorsport enthusiast, make a quick detour to Albert Park Lake, just 3 km south of the CBD. For most of the year, it's a calm, picturesque public park filled with black swans, walking trails, and rowers against the backdrop of the Melbourne skyline. But every autumn, its surrounding lakeside roads transform into the high-octane Albert Park Grand Prix Circuit for the Formula 1 Australian Grand Prix.

Free year-round access Outside race preparations, freely walk, run, or cycle the actual F1 track and pit lane

Fascinating history milestones

How tens of thousands of years of First Nations custodianship, colonial settlement, and gold rushes forged a proud, modern multicultural nation:

65,000+ BCE
The world's oldest living civilisations
Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples have inhabited and cared for Australia for more than 65,000 years across more than 250 distinct language nations. With oral songlines, the Dreamtime (creation cosmology), and rock art galleries older than the European cave paintings, Australia is home to humanity's longest continuous cultural heritage.
1770 & 1788
Cook's voyage & the First Fleet
In 1770, British Lieutenant James Cook sailed the eastern coastline aboard HMS Endeavour and claimed it for Britain. On 26 January 1788, Captain Arthur Phillip led the First Fleet of 11 ships carrying around 1,400 convicts, marines, and crew into Port Jackson (Sydney Cove), establishing the penal colony of New South Wales.
1851
The Gold Rush & the birth of democracy
Gold was struck in Ballarat and Bendigo (Victoria) and Bathurst (NSW), sparking a massive global gold rush that tripled Australia's population in ten years. In 1854, angry diggers revolted against oppressive colonial mining licences at the Eureka Stockade under the iconic Southern Cross Eureka flag. The rebellion was a defining turning point that accelerated democratic voting rights and secret ballots in Australia.
1901
Federation of the Commonwealth
On 1 January 1901, six separate self-governing British colonies officially federated to establish the unified Commonwealth of Australia. In 1902, Australia became one of the first countries in the world to grant women both the right to vote and to stand for national parliament.
1956 – Present
Multicultural renaissance & Olympic glory
Following post-World War II migration programs and the dismantling of the White Australia Policy, Australia welcomed millions of immigrants from Europe, Asia, the Americas, and Africa, creating one of the world's most vibrant multicultural societies. Australia cemented its global sporting stature by hosting the 1956 Melbourne Olympics, the unforgettable 2000 Sydney Olympics, and is preparing to host Brisbane 2032.

Official & territorial flags of Australia

Australia has three officially proclaimed national flags under the Flags Act 1953, along with distinctive flags for each of its six states and two mainland territories:

Proclaimed national flags
Australian National FlagEst. 1901
Features the Union Jack (British ties), the 7-pointed Commonwealth Star (representing the 6 states plus the territories), and the five stars of the Southern Cross constellation visible across the southern hemisphere.
Australian Aboriginal FlagEst. 1971
Designed by Harold Thomas. Black represents the Aboriginal people; red represents the sacred red earth and spiritual relation to Country; the central yellow disc represents the sun, giver of life and protector.
Torres Strait Islander FlagEst. 1992
Designed by Bernard Namok. Green represents the land; black stripes the people; blue the sea; the white Dhari (headdress) represents cultural heritage; and the 5-pointed star represents the 5 major island groups and navigation.
State & territory flags
New South Wales (NSW)Est. 1876
Blue ensign featuring the badge of NSW: St George's Cross with a golden lion passant at the centre and four golden stars on the arms.
Victoria (VIC)Est. 1877
Blue ensign with the badge of Victoria: the five stars of the Southern Cross constellation crowned with the Royal Crown of Saint Edward.
Queensland (QLD)Est. 1876
Blue ensign with the Queensland badge: a white roundel featuring a sky-blue Maltese cross surmounted by the Imperial Crown.
Western Australia (WA)Est. 1953
Blue ensign featuring a yellow roundel with Western Australia's state bird, the native black swan (Cygnus atratus), facing left.
South Australia (SA)Est. 1904
Blue ensign with a golden disc showing the native Piping Shrike (white-backed magpie) perched with outstretched wings on a gum branch.
Tasmania (TAS)Est. 1876
Blue ensign with a white disc featuring a red lion passant, symbolising historic loyalty to the British Crown and Tasmanian identity.
Australian Capital Territory (ACT)Est. 1993
Canberra's flag uses the territory colours of blue and gold, featuring the Southern Cross and the City of Canberra Coat of Arms.
Northern Territory (NT)Est. 1978
Black and rich ochre-red fields representing the outback soil, featuring the Southern Cross and the 7-petalled Sturt's Desert Rose.

Popular & iconic Australians

Trailblazers in wildlife conservation, sports, performing arts, and global scientific innovation who shaped Australia and inspired the world:

Steve Irwin (1962–2006)
The Crocodile Hunter & conservationist
Internationally adored wildlife champion and educator whose infectious energy, love for apex predators, and phrase "Crikey!" turned millions of people across the globe into passionate protectors of the natural world.
Cathy Freeman OAM
Olympic gold champion & cultural icon
Proud Kuku Yalanji sprinter who lit the Sydney 2000 Olympic cauldron and stormed to 400m gold in front of 112,000 cheering fans. Her victory lap holding both the Australian and Aboriginal flags became a landmark symbol of national unity.
Sir Donald Bradman (1908–2001)
"The Don" — cricket's greatest
Universally recognised as the greatest batsman in cricket history. Bradman finished his Test career with an astounding average of 99.94 — a statistical feat considered the most dominant record in any world sport.
Margot Robbie
Hollywood powerhouse & producer
Rising from regional Queensland to global fame, Robbie has emerged as one of cinema's most formidable talents, starring in and producing worldwide blockbusters like Barbie, I, Tonya, and The Wolf of Wall Street.
Chris Hemsworth & Hugh Jackman
Beloved actors & global ambassadors
World-famous for embodying iconic superheroes Thor and Wolverine, both Hemsworth and Jackman are renowned for their generous charity work, Broadway stage mastery, and down-to-earth Aussie humour.
AC/DC & Kylie Minogue
Rock & pop pioneers
AC/DC (formed in Sydney) defined hard rock with over 200 million records sold (Back in Black). Melbourne-born Kylie Minogue has dominated global pop music across five consecutive decades, proving Australia's musical prowess.
Howard Florey & Fiona Wood
World-saving medical innovators
Adelaide-born Nobel Laureate Howard Florey co-developed penicillin, saving an estimated 200 million lives. Western Australian surgeon Fiona Wood invented patented "spray-on skin" for severe burn victims worldwide.
Banjo Paterson (1864–1941)
Bush poet & cultural bard
Australia's beloved bush poet who penned the unofficial national anthem "Waltzing Matilda" and the legendary frontier ballad "The Man from Snowy River", capturing the timeless spirit of the Australian outback.

Did you know?

  • More beaches than days: Australia has 10,685 beaches. If you visited a new one every single day, it would take you more than 29 years.
  • Living giant: The Great Barrier Reef is the largest living structure on Earth, stretching over 2,300 km and visible from space.
  • The Dingo Fence: Stretches 5,614 km across South Australia, NSW, and Queensland — making it longer than the Great Wall of China.
  • Polymer banknotes: Australia invented the world's first waterproof polymer banknotes in 1988, now used by over 30 countries.
  • Unique wildlife: Over 80% of Australia's mammals, reptiles, and frogs are endemic — meaning you can't find wild kangaroos, koalas, platypuses, or wombats anywhere else on the planet.
